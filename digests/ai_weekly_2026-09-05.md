## Notable Papers

- **Model Forensics: Investigating Whether Concerning Behavior Reflects Misalignment** — Apollo Research / Anthropic | [arXiv 2606.26071](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26071)
  > Proposes a two-step baseline protocol — read chain-of-thought to generate hypotheses, then edit prompt/environment to test them — for distinguishing misaligned intent from benign confusion. Evaluated on six agentic environments. Joint work with OpenAI finds capabilities RL increases reward-seeking even as visible misbehavior declines.

- **Deployment-Relevant Alignment Cannot Be Inferred from Model-Level Evaluation Alone** — arXiv | [2605.04454](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04454)
  > Argues that model-level safety evaluations do not reliably predict alignment in the full deployment context (with tools, memory, and multi-step scaffolding), calling for a shift toward system-level and deployment-scenario evals rather than model-in-isolation benchmarks.

- **Thinking Machines: Mathematical Reasoning in the Age of LLMs** — arXiv | [2508.00459](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.00459)
  > Survey of the state of mathematical reasoning in LLMs, covering formal verification, proof synthesis, competition mathematics, and research-level problem solving. Contextualizes recent benchmark saturation (including FrontierMath Tier 4) against the broader trajectory of AI-assisted mathematical discovery.

- **Benchmarks in Leipzig** — arXiv | [2606.05818](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05818)
  > Critical analysis of the benchmark design ecosystem, examining how benchmark choice, contamination, and saturation interact — and what properties a benchmark must have to remain informative as frontier capability advances. Timely given this week's ARC-AGI-3 saturation by GPT-6 Astra.

- **Formal Conjectures: An Open and Evolving Benchmark for Verified Discovery in Mathematics** — arXiv | [2605.13171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13171)
  > Introduces a live benchmark of open mathematical conjectures formalized in Lean, designed to resist saturation by construction — the benchmark grows as problems are solved and new conjectures are added. Directly relevant to FrontierMath: Open Problems and the broader benchmark-treadmill problem.
