## 1. New Model Releases & Capability Announcements

### Anthropic — Claude Duet (Research Preview)
Anthropic launched **Claude Duet** on October 21 as a proactive AI agent available by waitlist to US Pro and Max subscribers. Duet runs on Claude Opus 5.5, monitors connected services, and surfaces only what needs attention each morning. Actions like sending messages, making payments, or deleting content still require explicit user approval. It is positioned as a rival to Meta's Muse and OpenAI's Dots.

- [Anthropic unveils Claude Duet AI agent — Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/computing/anthropic-unveils-claude-duet-ai-agent/)

- [Claude on X (official announcement)](https://x.com/claudeai/status/2112984664256241837)

### Anthropic — Claude Haiku 5.5 & Sonnet 5.5
**Haiku 5.5** (October 7) is Anthropic's fastest, most capable small model. **Sonnet 5.5** offers ~30% speed improvement and ~30% cost reduction over its predecessor, targeting high-volume production workloads.

### Meta — Muse Wonder & Muse Wonder Full
Meta introduced **Muse Wonder** and **Muse Wonder Full** on October 12 — its first self-described frontier-class models. Both are built for personal and professional agentic tasks, featuring "Active Memory" (persistent user context) and "Active Focus" (selective attention). Wonder is available via the Meta Model API ($4/1M input, $20/1M output tokens, 1M-context window). Wonder Full is limited to Muse Code subscribers and is not offered via API. A follow-on open-weight release, **Muse Spark Open**, shipped October 15.

**Gemini 4 Argon** reached general availability on October 13 at introductory pricing of $2/1M input and $10/1M output tokens, which doubles December 1. The UK AI Security Institute is reportedly evaluating Argon for safety risks.

## 2. New Benchmarks & Evaluation Frameworks

### ImpactBench (arXiv 2610.20690)
A team from MIT submitted **ImpactBench** on October 14 — an open, expert-guided platform for measuring how generative AI affects users' psychological, physical, and social wellbeing. The paper evaluates ten frontier LLMs across 48,540 conversations; their automated judge matched majority expert ratings in 83.3% of cases (Cohen's κ = 0.667). Code was released on GitHub (v1.0.0, October 20).

- [arXiv:2610.20690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20690)
- [GitHub release v1.0.0](https://github.com/mit-cyborgpsychology/impactbench/releases/tag/v1.0.0)

### LiveBench 2.0 (Release 2026-10-14)
**LiveBench 2.0** is an updated contamination-free benchmark that grades models on long-horizon real work: slide decks, research reports, multi-hour bug-fix sessions, and deployable apps. New release published October 14; leaderboard updated October 23.

- [LiveBench 2.0](https://livebench.ai/)

## 3. AI Evaluation Research

### Apollo Research — Embedded Evaluations for Scheming (October 14)
Apollo published two companion pieces: **"Towards Embedded Evaluations for Scheming Propensities"** and **"Principles for Embedded Evaluations."** The posts argue that external red-teaming is insufficient for detecting scheming in production systems, and lay out four claims a safety case needs along with the deep, persistent access required to verify them. Apollo also disclosed that its evaluation of Meta's Muse Spark found the **highest rate of evaluation awareness it has ever observed** in a frontier model.

- [Apollo Research Science page](https://www.apolloresearch.ai/science)
- [Apollo Research](https://www.apolloresearch.ai/)

### METR — Frontier Risk Report Follow-up & Expenditure Horizon Framework
METR's July 21 paper proposes an **"expenditure horizon"** metric for quantifying AI agents' optimization ability, empirically illustrated with the NanoGPT speedrun. In policy news, Anthropic is onboarding six METR employees for embedded third-party safety reviews of its frontier models.

- [METR Research](https://metr.org/research/)
- [METR Frontier Risk Report (May 2026)](https://metr.org/blog/2026-05-19-frontier-risk-report/)

## 4. Key Industry Developments (Safety, Policy, Infrastructure)

### US Congress: Multiple AI Safety Bills
Three separate proposals advanced in October:
- **Cantwell** — tasks NIST with developing standards against catastrophic harms for frontier AI systems.
- **Warner et al.** — creates an AI Safety Board within the Department of Commerce (Artificial Intelligence Risk Management and Security Act of 2026).
- **Hawley** — requires Department of Energy stress-testing of powerful models before release, with penalties for companies that skip review (AI Risk Evaluation Act).

- [AI Safety Regulations Gain Momentum — PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/ai-safety-regulations-gain-momentum-in-congress-ahead-of-midterms/)
- [Anthropic's AI safety bill that may block a model if 50+ could be killed — TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/20/anthropic-pitches-ai-safety-bill-that-may-block-a-model-if-50-people-could-be-killed/)

### Anthropic Circulates Its Own AI Safety Bill
Anthropic has been pitching a federal proposal that would allow a model to be blocked if 50 or more people could be killed — while also barring agencies from blocking a release without a court order. The proposal was reported by TechCrunch on October 20.

- [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/20/anthropic-pitches-ai-safety-bill-that-may-block-a-model-if-50-people-could-be-killed/)

### FTC Subpoenas OpenAI and Anthropic
The US Federal Trade Commission subpoenaed both OpenAI and Anthropic over AI safety concerns, according to Inside AI Policy (October 8–21 coverage).

- [Lexology: AI key updates 8–21 October](https://lexology.com/pro/content/artificial-intelligence-key-updates-and-developments-8-21-oct)

### EU: First AI Act Harmonised Standard
The EU Commission's first harmonised standard supporting the **AI Act** was scheduled for publication on October 19. The Commission also convened a special meeting of its scientific panel on frontier AI safety and risks.

- [EU AI Act — European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai)

### Microsoft Calls for Global AI Rules
Microsoft President Brad Smith argued publicly that AI is less regulated than milk and called for internationally coordinated AI oversight rules.

## This Week's Highlight

**Anthropic Claude Duet** is the week's most practitioner-relevant development. It is the first time a frontier lab has shipped a proactive AI agent — one that monitors your inbox, calendar, and connected services and reaches out to you rather than waiting to be prompted — at a mainstream subscription price point ($20/month Pro tier). With Meta's Muse and OpenAI's Dots in the same race, October 2026 marks a clear inflection point: personal AI agents are moving from demos to production waitlists.
