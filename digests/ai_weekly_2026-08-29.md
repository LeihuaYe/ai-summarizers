Evaluating open-weight options should now be a standard step in any infrastructure or product decision involving LLMs.

## Notable Papers

- **Can AI agents conduct open-ended AI research? Early evidence from two case studies** — arXiv | [2607.27191](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27191)
  > Shadow evals on two unpublished NeurIPS 2026 submissions gave frontier agents six days and thousands of dollars of compute. Agents completed all engineering tasks unassisted but failed to make substantial research progress. Five recurring failure modes identified: poor quality bar judgment, uncreative responses to design shortcomings, ineffective backtracking, poor resource awareness, and instruction drift.

- **An Empirical Study of Automating Agent Evaluation** — arXiv | [2605.11378](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11378)
  > Identifies evaluation as the primary bottleneck in agent deployment, arguing that agent evals must assess full execution traces (reasoning steps, tool invocations, error recovery, state transitions) rather than static output — and proposes automation strategies for scalable trajectory evaluation.

- **Open-World Evaluations for Measuring Frontier AI Capabilities** — arXiv | [2605.20520](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20520)
  > Proposes open-ended evaluation protocols that resist benchmark saturation by dynamically generating novel tasks, offering an alternative to fixed benchmark suites as frontier models begin plateauing on existing evals.

- **When AI Benchmarks Plateau: A Systematic Study of Benchmark Saturation** — arXiv | [2602.16763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16763)
  > Analysis of 60 LLM benchmarks using 14 saturation-related properties finds nearly half already exhibit saturation, with rates accelerating with benchmark age — quantifying the eval treadmill problem the field faces as frontier capability advances.

- **When Benchmark Inferences Do Not Compose: Projectibility in AI Evaluation** — arXiv | [2607.26159](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26159)
  > Examines when benchmark results validly generalize to new tasks and systems, finding that composition of benchmark inferences often fails — a fundamental challenge for using leaderboard rankings to predict real-world capability.
