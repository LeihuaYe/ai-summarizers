## 3. Notable AI Evaluation Research

- **"Can AI agents conduct open-ended AI research?" (arXiv 2607.27191)** — arxiv.org | https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27191
  > Narayanan et al. introduced "shadow evaluations" — giving frontier agents the open-ended research question of two unpublished NeurIPS 2026 submissions, six days, and thousands of dollars of compute — and found agents completed all engineering tasks autonomously but failed to make substantial progress on the open-ended research questions, identifying five recurring failure modes; a significant negative result for current "AI-accelerates-AI-research" forecasts.

- **"Agent Identity Evals: Measuring Agentic Identity" (arXiv 2507.17257)** — arxiv.org | https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17257
  > Perrier & Bennett introduce a statistically-driven framework for measuring whether LMA systems maintain stable, consistent identity over time, addressing how statelessness, stochasticity, and prompt sensitivity undermine agent reliability and trustworthiness in long-running deployments.

- **METR Frontier Risk Report (Feb–Mar 2026)** — metr.org | https://metr.org/blog/2026-05-19-frontier-risk-report/
  > The first industry-wide misalignment risk assessment, run with Anthropic, Google, Meta, and OpenAI, found agents were highly capable on "hill-climbable" engineering tasks (reimplementation, vulnerability discovery) but that none of the evaluated models showed significantly greater autonomous capabilities than their publicly documented counterparts; METR plans to repeat the exercise later in 2026.

- **International AI Safety Report 2026** — arxiv.org | https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.21012
  > A comprehensive multi-stakeholder report synthesizing the current state of frontier AI risks, capabilities, and governance gaps, used as a key reference for policymakers heading into the EU AI Act enforcement period.
